Crazy Breeze is a piano on its own, no drums: three four-chord loops in E minor,
**Em – G – D – Am**, **Em – D – C – D** and **G – Am – B – Em**, first as chords over a low
piano bass, then as quick broken chords. The 8-bar song plays three times, and each time the
last bar sounds darker.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `const melody_1 = "<Em G D Am>"` | One of the three chord loops; `< >` takes the next chord each time round. |
| `.dict("triads").mode("above").anchor("<E4 D4 D4 C4>")` | Plain three-note chords (triads), built upward from the anchor note, so the hand stays in one place. |
| `n("0 <[7 3] [7 4] [7 4] [7 3]>")` | Which notes of the chord play: `0` the lowest, `3`, `4` and `7` higher ones. In `piano_2`, `[7, 3]` with a comma plays two at once. |
| `note("<[E1 G1] [D1 A1]>")` | The low bass of the opening: E and G in the first bar, D and A in the second, one octave below the `E2 G2` / `D2 A2` line it doubles. Each is the root (the note a chord is named after) of the chord above it: Em, G, D, Am. |
| `n("0 <[1 2 3] …>")` | The last three bars: the chord climbs note by note (`1 2 3`) instead of sounding together. |
| `.fast(2)` … `.fast(8)` | The same chords at 2×, 4× and 8× speed: from chords to running notes. |
| `.lpf("<2000 800 300>")` | A low-pass filter on the last bar: it lets only sounds below 2000, then 800, then 300 Hz (vibrations per second) through, so the bar gets darker on each of the three passes. |
| `arrange([2, intro_1], [1, piano_2], [2, outro_1], [1, piano_21], [1, piano_22], [1, piano_23])` | The 8-bar song (2 + 1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1). It loops; the recording plays it three times: 24 bars. |
